# The Power of Proverbs

**Acting First**

- The early bird catches the worm — moving first gives you the best chance.
- First come, first served — the person who arrives first gets the place.
- You snooze, you lose — if you wait too long, you miss your chance.
- Strike while the iron is hot — act now, while the moment is good.

**Planning and Risk**

- Don't count your chickens before they hatch — don't plan on good news too soon.
- Don't put all your eggs in one basket — don't risk everything on one plan.
- Better safe than sorry — it is wiser to be careful now than to regret later.
- Look before you leap — check the risks before you jump into something.

**Judging and Comparing**

- Don't judge a book by its cover — looks can be misleading.
- The grass is always greener on the other side — other lives look better.
- Actions speak louder than words — what people do matters more than words.
- It takes two to tango — in an argument, both people share the blame.

**Time and Effort**

- Rome wasn't built in a day — big things take time and patience.
- Time flies when you're having fun — good times seem to pass quickly.
- No use crying over spilt milk — don't stay upset about what already happened.
- What goes around comes around — how you treat others comes back to you.

**Discussion**

- Which of these proverbs do you actually use? Which sound old-fashioned?
- Is the early bird always right? When is waiting smarter than rushing?
- Tell us about a time you counted your chickens a little too soon.
- "Don't put all your eggs in one basket" — what are your baskets right now?
- Have you ever judged a book by its cover and been wrong? What happened?
- Why do other people's lives always look greener on the other side?
- Do you know someone whose actions speak louder than their words?
- What have you been building slowly, like Rome? Are you still at it?
- When did time fly for you this year? What were you doing?
- What is spilt milk in your life — something you had to stop crying over?
- Do you believe "what goes around comes around"? Give a real example.
- Would you rather be safe than sorry, or take a big risk? Why?
- Which proverb would you teach a child first, and why?
- Do proverbs still help people, or are they just old sayings?
