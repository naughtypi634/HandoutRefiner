# Communication Skills

**The Communication Process**

- Sender — the person who starts the message
- Message — verbal or non-verbal, explicit or implicit
- Channel — the medium: spoken words, written text, gestures, or visuals
- Receiver — the person the message is meant for
- Feedback — understanding, agreement, disagreement, or any other response
- Noise — anything that interferes: distractions, language and culture gaps, or bias

**Communication Styles**

- Assertive — direct and respectful; clear, and open to hearing other views
- Aggressive — dominates, ignores others, and uses hostile or confrontational words
- Passive — avoids conflict, hesitant, and struggles to set boundaries
- Passive-aggressive — indirect: sarcasm, small insults, or sulking instead of saying it
- Diplomatic — keeps the peace, chooses words carefully, and looks for compromise
- Collaborative — cooperation, active listening, and a solution both sides accept
- Non-verbal — body language, facial expressions, gestures, and tone of voice

**Communication Strategy**

- Define objectives — specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, time-bound
- Identify your audience — their needs, preferences, and how they like to be reached
- Craft key messages — consistent, compelling, and easy to understand
- Select channels — meetings, email, WeChat, written notice, or face to face
- Set a timeline — with clear milestones and deadlines
- Monitor and evaluate — collect feedback, track KPIs, and adjust as you go
- Make it two-way — invite questions and responses from your audience
- Consider culture and diversity — the same words can land differently

**Discussion**

- Which of these styles is closest to your own? Give a recent example.
- When do you switch styles — with your boss, your team, or a client?
- Have you ever worked with an aggressive communicator? How did you handle it?
- Is being diplomatic always better than being direct?
- When did a message you sent online get misunderstood? What went wrong?
- How do you give bad news to a client without losing them?
- What do you do when a meeting keeps going off topic?
- How does culture change the way people say no at work?
- What is the hardest conversation you have had at work?
- Who in your team communicates best, and what makes them good at it?
