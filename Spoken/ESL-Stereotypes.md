Stereotypes

**What Are Stereotypes?**

Stereotypes are oversimplified beliefs or assumptions about a group of
people based on characteristics like gender, age, occupation, or region.
They often ignore individual differences and can lead to unfair
judgments.

- People who wear glasses must be smart.

- Tall people must be good at basketball.

- Cat people are quiet; dog people are outgoing.

- Left-handed people are more creative.

- Only children are spoiled.

**Is there a good stereotype?**

For example:

- "Asians are good at math."

- "Teachers are nurturing."

- "Nurses are patient and caring."

- "French people are romantic."

- "Women are better listeners."

**Personal Reflection: A Stereotype That Wasn't True**

Think of a stereotype you once believed, like "Everyone from Sichuan
eats spicy food."

- I used to think programmers are all quiet and bad at small talk, but my
  cousin joined a tech company last year and now he never stops talking at
  family dinners.

- I used to think people over 50 can't learn new technology, but my mom
  learned to pay with her phone, call a ride, and post short videos — all
  in one year.

- I used to think only children are spoiled and selfish, until I met my
  friend Liu, an only child who cooks for his parents every weekend.

**Different types of stereotypes**

- Gender

Family roles: Men are usually the providers; women are usually the
  caregivers.

Personality: Women are more emotional; men are more rational.

Hobbies: Football is for boys; dancing is for girls.

Behavior: Boys don't cry.

Skills and abilities: Men are better at math and driving; women are
  better at languages.

Occupations: Nurses and kindergarten teachers are mostly women.

- Age

Seniors: Seniors can't use smartphones.

Teenagers: Teenagers are lazy and only play games.

Kids: Kids are honest and innocent.

- Occupation

Teachers: Teachers are patient and love to talk.

Doctors: Doctors are cold and always in a hurry.

- Region

North: Northerners are tall and good at drinking.

South: Southerners are good at doing business.

Certain provinces: People from Sichuan all eat spicy food.

Certain countries: French people are romantic.

- Brands

People who buy Pop Mart blind boxes love surprises.

Moutai is for business dinners, not casual meals.

People who shop at Sam's Club always buy in bulk.

**Where did we get the stereotypes?**

- **Media**: Movies, TV, and short videos repeat the same picture —
  scientists are always nerdy guys with glasses, and the boss in every
  short drama is cold, rich, and never smiles.

- **School and teachers**: Textbooks and teachers oversimplify — "people
  in rich countries all live easy lives" or "students from poor families
  study harder."

- **People around us**: Family and friends repeat them — aunts at family
  dinners say a woman who isn't married by 30 must be too picky.

- **Our own brain**: We take shortcuts — putting people in boxes is easier
  than getting to know every single person.
