# Meetings: Useful or a Waste of Time

**Meeting Vocabulary**

**Meeting Types**

- agenda (noun): the list of topics for a meeting.
- briefing (noun): a short meeting that gives people important information.
- one-to-one (noun): a private meeting between two people.
- team meeting (noun): a meeting for people who work together.
- video conference (noun): a meeting held online with video.
- workshop (noun): a meeting where people work together on a task or problem.
- scheduled meeting (noun): a meeting planned for a specific time.
- ad hoc meeting (noun): a meeting arranged when a new issue comes up.

**Useful Meeting Expressions**

- set the agenda (verb phrase): decide what the meeting will cover.
- take minutes (verb phrase): write a record of what people say and decide.
- give an update (verb phrase): explain the latest progress.
- raise an issue (verb phrase): bring an important problem to the group's attention.
- make a decision (verb phrase): choose what the group will do.
- reach an agreement (verb phrase): agree on a plan or answer.
- stay on track (verb phrase): continue to follow the agenda and time plan.
- follow up on something (verb phrase): take the next action after a meeting.
- run over (phrasal verb): continue longer than the planned time.
- wrap up (phrasal verb): finish a meeting or discussion.

**Warm-up Discussion**

- What kind of work do you do, and what kinds of meetings do you have?

- How often do you have meetings, and how long does a typical one last?

- What are your meetings usually about: updates, planning, problem-solving, or something else?

- What makes a meeting feel too long?

- What is one meeting that was useful? What made it useful?

- What is one meeting that felt like a waste of time? What went wrong?

**Discussion**

- What role do you usually play in a meeting? Do you lead, present, take notes, ask questions, or listen?

- How can you contribute to a meeting if you do not have much to say?

- Do you prefer in-person or virtual meetings? What is one advantage of your choice?

- When is an ad hoc meeting helpful, and when does it create extra stress?

- What meeting manners matter most, such as arriving on time, muting your microphone, or not interrupting?

- How can a chairperson keep a meeting focused and stop it from running over?

- When should a team send an email instead of holding a meeting?

- Is it okay to eat or drink during a meeting? When might it be acceptable?

- What should happen after a meeting so that decisions become real actions?

- What makes a meeting necessary rather than a waste of time?
