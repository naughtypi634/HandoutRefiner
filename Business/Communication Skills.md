# What is COMMUNICATION?

- Sender

- Message: It can be verbal or non-verbal, **[explicit]{.underline}** or
  **[implicit]{.underline}**.

- Channel: The **[medium]{.underline}** such as spoken words, written
  text, gestures, or visual aids.

- Receiver

- Feedback: understanding, agreement, disagreement, or any other
  relevant response.

- Noise: Any **[interference]{.underline}** or **[barrier]{.underline}**
  that may disrupt or distort the communication process. This can
  include **[physical distractions]{.underline}**, **[language
  barriers]{.underline}**, **[cultural differences]{.underline}**, or
  even **[internal biases]{.underline}**.

# What's your communication style/strategy?

***[Style:]{.underline}***

- Assertive: express thoughts, feelings, and needs in a
  **[direct]{.underline}** and **[respectful]{.underline}** manner.
  confident, clear, and open to listening to others. They strive for a
  win-win outcome in conversations and value open dialogue.

- Aggressive: tend to **[dominate]{.underline}** conversations,
  **[disregard]{.underline}** others\' opinions, and use confrontational
  or hostile language. They often **[prioritize]{.underline}** their own
  needs and may not consider the feelings or perspectives of others.

- Passive: Passive communicators **[avoid conflict]{.underline}** and
  tend to be **[hesitant]{.underline}** in expressing their thoughts or
  needs. They may struggle with **[asserting]{.underline}** themselves
  and often prioritize others\' opinions over their own. Passive
  communicators may have difficulty **[setting boundaries]{.underline}**
  or **[advocating for]{.underline}** themselves.

- Passive-Aggressive: Passive-aggressive communicators indirectly
  express their dissatisfaction or disagreement. They may use
  **[sarcasm]{.underline}**, **[subtle insults]{.underline}**, or
  non-verbal cues to convey their true feelings. This style can create
  **[tension]{.underline}** and **[confusion]{.underline}** in
  communication.

- Diplomatic: Diplomatic communicators aim to **[maintain
  harmony]{.underline}** and positive relationships. They choose their
  words carefully, consider others\' perspectives, and **[seek
  compromise]{.underline}**. They often use tact and empathy to navigate
  sensitive or challenging conversations.

- Collaborative: Collaborative communicators emphasize cooperation,
  active listening, and problem-solving. They value
  **[input]{.underline}** from **[all parties]{.underline}** involved
  and seek to find mutually beneficial solutions. Collaboration fosters
  teamwork and encourages open communication.

- Non-Verbal: Non-verbal communication styles involve expressing oneself
  through **[body language]{.underline}**, **[facial
  expressions]{.underline}**, **[gestures]{.underline}**, and **[tone of
  voice]{.underline}**. Non-verbal cues can greatly impact the message
  being conveyed and can either **[support]{.underline}** or
  **[contradict]{.underline}** verbal communication.

***[Strategy:]{.underline}***

- Define objectives: specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and
  time-bound (SMART).

- Identify target audience: understand their needs, preferences, and
  communication preferences.

- Craft key messages: These messages should be consistent, compelling,
  and easily understood.

- Select channels: Meetings, email, websites, written notice, or
  face-to-face interactions, etc.

- Timeline: including specific milestones and deadlines.

- Monitor and evaluate: Regularly monitor and evaluate the effectiveness
  of your communication strategy. Collect feedback, measure key
  performance indicators (KPIs), and adjust accordingly.
  PDCA(Plan-Do-Check-Action)

- Two-way communication: Encourage feedback, questions, and dialogue
  from your audience.

- Consider cultural and diversity factors
