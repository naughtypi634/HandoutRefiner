# Digital Nomads

**Why People Choose It**

- Freedom and flexibility — work from anywhere with internet: a big city, a beach, or a mountain town.
- Control over your schedule — set your own hours and work when you are most productive.
- Cultural enrichment — living in new countries opens your eyes and sparks new ideas.
- Lower costs — many nomad hubs cost far less to live in than a big home city.
- New connections — you meet people from everywhere and grow your network.

**How to Start**

- Check your skills — programming, design, marketing, writing and teaching English all travel well.
- Find remote work — negotiate with your boss, freelance, or start an online business.
- Plan your money — budget for travel, rent, insurance and daily costs, and keep an emergency fund.
- Research the places — compare cost of living, safety, visa rules and internet speed.
- Sort the logistics — flights, an apartment, health insurance, and tax rules back home.
- Pack light — traveling often means few things, so choose light and multi-use items.
- Stay connected — good internet matters for both work calls and keeping in touch.
- Protect your health — keep exercising, eat well, and set work hours so the job does not take over.

**Where to Go**

- Estonia — a Digital Nomad Visa lets certain workers stay and work for up to a year.
- Germany — the freelance visa is a route many remote workers use.
- Spain — a visa that lets you live there while working for a foreign company.
- Portugal — a popular hub with a visa for people on a steady income.
- Costa Rica — a visa for people who have a regular income from abroad.
- Mexico — a temporary resident visa you can renew for several more years.
- Georgia — a program that lets remote workers stay for at least 180 days.
- Barbados — a welcome stamp that lets you live and work there for a year.
