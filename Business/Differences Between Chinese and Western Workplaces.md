# Differences Between Chinese and Western Workplaces

**Company Culture**

- West: bosses allow banter and let staff manage their own time.
- West: music, breaks, chat, and browsing are fine if work gets done.
- West: deadlines are tight and things are expected to move fast.
- China: more emphasis on protocol, respect for authority, and close bonds.
- China: staff make the office feel like home — plants and personal items.
- China: team lunches and formal team-building build a family feel.
- China: business moves more slowly, with patience over speed.

**Roles and Responsibilities**

- West: roles are clearly defined and handed to each person.
- West: each person finishes their own tasks and shows their own ability.
- West: helping outside your area can look like "stepping on toes".
- China: your role often stretches beyond your job description.
- China: your boss decides what extra work you take on.
- China: everyone pitches in — it shows commitment to the job.

**Overtime**

- West: hours are flexible as long as the work gets done.
- West: most people head home at 5 or 6 p.m.
- West: extra hours are paid back in pay, time off, or flexi-time.
- China: leaving right at the end of the day is unusual.
- China: many stay late, unpaid, to show willing and impress the boss.

**Tardiness**

- West: being late can usually be made up at the end of the day.
- West: repeat offenders get a few warnings before real action.
- China: punctuality is a key sign of commitment.
- China: some companies dock pay each time you're late.

**Hierarchy and Layout**

- West: flatter structures and an "open door" to managers.
- West: you can raise work or personal problems with your manager.
- China: many layers, and pay grade shapes who has power.
- China: the more senior the person, the less often you see them.
- China: staff rarely joke with or share personal life with superiors.

**Meetings**

- West: quick, informal meetings to spark ideas.
- West: coffee-shop meetings, stand-ups, even plank meetings.
- West: everyone gives feedback, whatever their level.
- China: meetings are formal, official business (开会).
- China: expect minutes, a big agenda, and a long block of time.
- China: senior staff lead the talk; others speak only when asked.

**Relationships**

- Build business relationships slowly, after people get to know you.
- Contact usually comes face to face, not by phone or email.
- "Guanxi" — good relationships — matters more than a quick deal.
- Workers are expected to be friendly with the boss and socialize after work.
- Keep business and social life separate; don't talk business at meals.

**Greetings and Communication**

- Bow or nod; only shake hands if a hand is offered.
- Use formal titles and surnames when you address people.
- Applause is common when greeting a crowd.
- Book appointments in advance.
- Avoid staring into someone's eyes — it can seem disrespectful.

**Behavior**

- Be on time; arriving late is an insult.
- Avoid big hand movements.
- Don't point with your index finger — use an open palm.
- Avoid physical contact, like patting a colleague on the back.

**Clothing**

- Dress conservatively and simply.
- Men: dark suits and dark, formal ties.
- Women: suits or dresses with a high neckline.
- Low or flat heels are preferred over high heels.
- Avoid bright colors.
