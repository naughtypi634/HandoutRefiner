# Stereotypes: True or Not?

**What is a stereotype?**

A stereotype is a fixed, simple idea about a group of people. For example:
"All programmers wear hoodies and hate small talk," or "All northerners are
good at drinking."

Stereotypes are not always negative, but they ignore individual differences
and can lead to unfair judgments.

**Stereotypes in real life**

Do you recognize these? Which ones have you actually heard?

- Sichuan people all love spicy food.
- Northerners are tall and good at drinking.
- Teachers are patient and always ready to help.
- Programmers are quiet, introverted, and wear the same hoodie every day.
- Gen Z is addicted to phones and does not want to work hard.
- A woman who is not married by 30 must be "too picky."
- Men should pay on a first date.
- People from Guangdong will eat anything that has legs, except the table.

**Have you ever been stereotyped?**

- What stereotype have you heard about your hometown, your generation, or
  your job?
- Did it match your own experience? Why or why not?
- How did it make you feel, and what did you say or do?

**Where do stereotypes come from?**

- **Media**: Movies and short videos repeat the same images — for example,
  scientists are always nerdy guys with glasses.
- **Parents and school**: Children hear things like "Boys are better at
  science" or "Girls are not good at math."
- **Our own brain**: We categorize people to make quick decisions. It saves
  time, but it also skips the facts.
- **Algorithms**: Short-video apps keep showing us content that agrees with
  what we already believe.

**Can a stereotype be positive?**

- "Asians are good at math." Is this a compliment, or does it put pressure
  on kids?
- "Teachers are warm and caring." What happens when a teacher is not?
- What is the difference between "My colleague Li is good with numbers" and
  "All Chinese people are good with numbers"?

**Personal reflection: a stereotype that wasn't true**

- Think of a stereotype you once believed. When did it change?
- Example: "I used to think everyone from Sichuan loves spicy food — until
  my classmate Xiao Wang, who is from Chengdu, ordered a mild hotpot and
  asked for a glass of milk."
- Example: "I used to think people over 50 can't learn new technology, but
  my mom learned to pay with her phone, call a ride, and post short videos
  — all in one year."
- Use this frame: "I used to think ____, but then I met ____, and ____."

**What can we do about stereotypes?**

- A friend says, "Gen Z only wants to lie flat (躺平) and play games."
  What do you say?
- The next time you catch yourself thinking, "All ____ are like that," what
  can you do before you speak?
- Have you ever changed your mind about a group of people? What changed it?
- Is it ever okay to generalize? Where is the line between a stereotype and
  a useful first impression?

**Useful language**

- "That's a stereotype. Not all ____ are like that."
- "I used to think ____, but then I met ____."
- "There might be some truth to it, but it doesn't apply to everyone."
- "It's not fair to judge someone just because of ____."
- "To be honest, I was guilty of that, too."
